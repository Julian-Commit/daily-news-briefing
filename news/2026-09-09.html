DOE Closes a $1.9 Billion Loan to Restart the Duane Arnold Nuclear Plant; Google Buys Its 615 MW on a 25-Year Deal

US DOE · POWER Magazine · Utility Dive · Engadget · Gizmodo · 2026-09-08

The US Department of Energy's Office of Energy Dominance Financing announced on September 8 the financial close of a loan of up to $1.9 billion to NextEra Energy to finance the restart of the Duane Arnold Energy Center in Linn County, Iowa. The 615 MW plant ceased operations in August 2020 after a windstorm with gusts of about 140 mph destroyed its cooling towers, and had already been slated for decommissioning. NextEra holds a 25-year power purchase agreement with Alphabet's Google, which will take the output for its AI data centres. Commercial operation is targeted for the first quarter of 2029, subject to licensing approvals from the US Nuclear Regulatory Commission. The DOE said the project is expected to create roughly 1,500 construction jobs and support more than 450 permanent operating positions, and that the restarted plant would generate enough electricity for nearly 500,000 homes. Nepal's Bhotekoshi Flood Toll Rises to at Least 1,377 Dead and 5,326 Missing; Government Puts Rebuilding at $4–5 Billion

The Kathmandu Post · Reuters · Bloomberg · CNBC · ABC News · UNFPA · 2026-09-08

The Kathmandu Post reported on September 8 that the Bhotekoshi flood — set off on August 26 by a glacier collapse on the northern slopes of Langtang Lirung — has left at least 1,377 people confirmed dead, 5,326 missing and 6,827 injured, with damage concentrated in Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading districts. The figures have shifted repeatedly: on September 2, ABC News cited Nepal's National Disaster Management Authority for 1,114 dead and 3,916 missing inside Nepal, while Chinese officials reported 16 dead and about 546 missing on the Tibetan side. On financing, Nepal's finance minister told Reuters that reconstruction would require roughly $4 billion to $5 billion. The National Planning Commission has begun a Post-Disaster Needs Assessment, with a report expected within five weeks that will form the basis for a donor conference. Kathmandu plans to seek support from the global Loss and Damage Fund on the grounds that climate change worsened the flood's severity; Bloomberg and The Japan Times note the fund is new, underfunded and untested. On infrastructure, 48 suspension bridges were damaged, 28 of which need full replacement, and the government initially allocated about $6.5 million. More than 13,583 people have been rescued in total, and Nepal observed a national day of mourning on September 7. Inference Chips and Their Electricity: Two Kinds of Capacity Locked In on September 8

Sources: CNBC · Bloomberg · Reuters · TipRanks · Quartz · DigiTimes · TechCrunch · US DOE · POWER Magazine · Utility Dive

Two contracts on the same day

Two interlocking items landed on September 8: Qualcomm agreed with Amazon Web Services on multiple generations of custom AI inference silicon, and the US Department of Energy reached financial close on a $1.9 billion loan to restart a 615 MW nuclear plant whose entire output has already been bought by Google under a 25-year power purchase agreement. Compute on one side, electricity on the other — both contracted on timescales measured in decades.

1. The hyperscalers' in-house silicon list

Google's TPU and Amazon's Trainium and Inferentia are the established path. At Meta, Reuters and CNBC report that the in-house MTIA accelerator programme spans four planned generations; the fourth, code-named Iris, was designed with help from Broadcom and is to be built by TSMC, with production planned from September 2026. According to an internal memo, the chip completed critical testing in roughly six weeks, faster than the multi-month validation typical for comparable parts. On capacity, Meta says it will bring 7 gigawatts online in 2026 and double that to 14 gigawatts in 2027, and has signed long-term supply agreements for memory, storage and optical equipment. Qualcomm's agreement is its first data-centre win with a Western hyperscaler.

2. The structure of Qualcomm's deal

What stands out is the structure rather than the headline number. Per regulatory filings, Qualcomm issued Amazon warrants for 25 million shares at $161.26 each, worth about $4 billion. Of those, 3.75 million vested immediately against initial purchase commitments; the rest vest against further commercial commitments, with vesting capped against as much as $60 billion in orders. The warrant allows cashless exercise and expires in 2036. In effect, the supplier offers its own equity as part of the consideration for the customer's orders: the more the customer buys, the more equity it earns. On the technical side, the two stated elements are inference-specific silicon and in-cluster optical interconnect at up to 1.6 terabits per second. Qualcomm says the chips are already in production, with revenue beginning in its fiscal first quarter of 2027 — the December 2026 quarter.

3. The electricity side

The Duane Arnold Energy Center shut down in August 2020 after a windstorm destroyed its cooling towers, and had been slated for decommissioning. According to the DOE and POWER Magazine, NextEra Energy secured a loan of up to $1.9 billion, targets commercial operation in the first quarter of 2029 and still needs Nuclear Regulatory Commission approval; the 615 MW output goes to Alphabet's Google under a 25-year power purchase agreement for AI data centres. A unit idle for six years has been written back into the plan because of data-centre demand.

4. The state-level compute ledger

For context: China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology published its 15th Five-Year Plan for the information and communications industry this week, targeting 9,800 EFLOPS of intelligent computing capacity by 2030 — more than four times the current level — and 3.8 trillion yuan (about $532 billion) of cumulative investment in information infrastructure from 2026 to 2030. Reported capacity stood at 2,185 EFLOPS at the end of June and about 2,450 EFLOPS at the end of July, and the plan emphasises domestically produced processors.

5. What is not yet verifiable

Neither the part numbers nor the deployment schedule nor the volume of the Qualcomm-AWS silicon has been disclosed; the $60 billion figure is the ceiling against which the warrant vests, not the value of signed purchase contracts. Meta's Iris production timing rests on an internal memo and supply-chain reporting, and Meta has made no public disclosure on yields or shipment volumes. Duane Arnold's restart date depends on NRC review, and no final timeline has been set.

兰塘里隆掉下来的那块冰：波特科西洪水两周后的核算、归因与预警

综合信源：The Kathmandu Post · Bloomberg · The Japan Times · CNN · Al Jazeera · ABC News · CNBC · SciDev.Net · Down To Earth · ICIMOD · UNFPA

8月26日发生了什么

8月26日，尼泊尔拉苏瓦（Rasuwa）县的伦德（Lhende）—波特科西（Bhotekoshi）河谷发生破坏性洪水。据卫星影像分析，尼中边境附近高海拔处一大块冰川与周围岩体崩落，冰、雪与碎屑倾入河谷。维基百科汇总的技术描述为：一块约0.2平方公里（49英亩）的冰岩体在约5.2公里海拔处断裂，下落约1.2公里，形成强大的碎屑流；被记录到的地震信号来自崩塌本身，而非由地震触发。碎屑流沿河向下推进达100公里，有遗体在下游240公里的印度境内被发现。

卫星记录里的前史

同一河系在2025年7月8日已发生过一次性质不同的事件。据Sentinel-2影像，西藏一侧普日普（Purepu）冰川的冰面湖自2025年3月起形成并扩大，到7月7日合并湖面约0.75平方公里；7月8日湖面骤减、冰面出现排水特征，支持「冰面湖快速排水导致下游洪水」的判断。当时受影响的包括道路、桥梁、水电站与拉苏瓦加迪（Rasuwagadhi）陆港。同一走廊两年两次洪水，但两次的物理过程并不相同。

伤亡与损失的口径变化

灾情数字一直在变动：8月28日半岛电视台报道近470人死亡；9月1日死亡人数越过1,000人；9月2日美国广播公司援引尼泊尔国家灾害管理局为尼境内1,114人死亡、3,916人失踪；9月8日《加德满都邮报》报道至少1,377人死亡、5,326人失踪、6,827人受伤。西藏一侧，中国官方通报16人死亡、约546人失踪，维基百科汇总的9月5至6日数字为43人死亡、519人失踪。累计救援超过13,583人，9月4日至5日有两名幸存者从水电站隧道中被救出；至9月4日，多数通信基站恢复；9月7日尼泊尔举行全国哀悼日。

国际山地综合发展中心（ICIMOD）表示，伦德河的冰崩是嫌疑触发因素，但确切的事件顺序以及气候变化在此次灾害中的作用仍在调查中。ICIMOD 2026年的评估显示，兴都库什—喜马拉雅地区冰川在1990年至2020年间面积损失约12%，冰损速率自2000年以来翻了一倍；随着冰川退缩，新湖可能形成，坡体失去冰的支撑，冻土也可能变得不稳定。特里布万大学地质灾害专家巴桑塔·拉杰·阿迪卡里（Basanta Raj Adhikari）称8月26日的洪水在当地前所未见，并称其释放的能量超过广岛原子弹。雷丁大学的莉兹·斯蒂芬斯（Liz Stephens）将此类事件描述为「包括滑坡、雪崩与冰湖溃决洪水在内的复杂灾害链」。ICIMOD减灾专家萨斯瓦塔·桑亚尔（Saswata Sanyal）称：「随着本地区灾害风险的性质迅速变化，减灾战略已不能再孤立地评估单一灾种。」尼泊尔卫星科学家伊什法克·艾哈迈德（Ishfaq Ahmad）指出，危险冰川湖可以借卫星影像识别，但由于触发过程迅速，锁定溃决的确切时刻仍然困难。

The Ice That Fell from Langtang Lirung: Accounting, Attribution and Early Warning Two Weeks After the Bhotekoshi Flood

Sources: The Kathmandu Post · Bloomberg · The Japan Times · CNN · Al Jazeera · ABC News · CNBC · SciDev.Net · Down To Earth · ICIMOD · UNFPA

What happened on August 26

On August 26 a destructive flood swept the Lhende-Bhotekoshi corridor in Nepal's Rasuwa district. Satellite imagery analysis indicates that a large section of glacier and surrounding rock broke away at high altitude near the Nepal-China border, sending ice, snow and debris into the valley. A technical description compiled on Wikipedia puts it this way: a piece of ice and rock of about 0.2 square kilometres (49 acres) detached at roughly 5.2 kilometres elevation and fell some 1.2 kilometres, generating a powerful debris flow; the seismic signal that was recorded came from the collapse itself rather than from an earthquake that triggered it. The debris flow travelled up to 100 kilometres downstream, and bodies were recovered 240 kilometres away in India.

The prior history in the satellite record

The same river system produced a different kind of event on July 8, 2025. According to Sentinel-2 imagery, supraglacial ponds began forming on the Purepu Glacier on the Tibetan side in March 2025 and expanded, reaching a combined lake area of about 0.75 square kilometres by July 7; on July 8 the lake area fell sharply and drainage features became visible on the glacier surface, supporting the conclusion that rapid drainage of the supraglacial lake caused the downstream flood. Roads, bridges, hydroelectric plants and the Rasuwagadhi dry port were affected then. Two floods in two years in the same corridor — but not by the same physical process.

How the casualty figures have moved

The numbers have shifted repeatedly. On August 28 Al Jazeera reported nearly 470 dead; by September 1 the toll passed 1,000; on September 2 ABC News cited Nepal's National Disaster Management Authority for 1,114 dead and 3,916 missing inside Nepal; on September 8 The Kathmandu Post reported at least 1,377 dead, 5,326 missing and 6,827 injured. On the Tibetan side, Chinese officials reported 16 dead and about 546 missing, while figures compiled on Wikipedia for September 5-6 give 43 dead and 519 missing. More than 13,583 people have been rescued in total; two survivors were pulled from hydropower tunnels on September 4 and 5; most telecommunications towers had been restored by September 4; and Nepal observed a national day of mourning on September 7.

Attribution: what is settled and what is still under investigation

The International Centre for Integrated Mountain Development (ICIMOD) says an ice avalanche in the Lhende Khola is the suspected trigger, but that the precise sequence of events and the role of climate change in this particular disaster remain under investigation. ICIMOD's 2026 assessment found that glaciers across the Hindu Kush Himalaya lost about 12% of their area between 1990 and 2020, with the rate of ice loss doubling since 2000; as glaciers retreat, new lakes can form, slopes lose the support ice provided, and frozen ground can become unstable. Basanta Raj Adhikari, a geohazard specialist at Tribhuvan University, described the August 26 flood as unprecedented locally and said the energy released exceeded that of the Hiroshima atomic bomb. Liz Stephens of the University of Reading described such events as “complex chains of hazards, including landslides, avalanches and glacial lake outburst floods.” Saswata Sanyal, an ICIMOD disaster-risk reduction specialist, said: “With the nature of disaster risks rapidly mutating in the region, disaster risk reduction strategies can no longer evaluate hazards in isolation.” Ishfaq Ahmad, a satellite scientist in Nepal, notes that dangerous glacial lakes can be identified from satellite imagery but that pinpointing the moment of failure remains difficult because triggers act quickly.

Early warning and those four glacial lakes

Nepal is advancing a project called GLOF Sanjeevani to lower water levels at four high-risk glacial lakes in the Koshi and Gandaki basins: Lumding Tsho and Hongu-2 in Solukhumbu, Lower Barun in Sankhuwasabha and Thulagi (Dona) in Manang. The budget is about $50 million, of which $36.16 million is a Green Climate Fund grant and $13.8 million comes from the government and partners, with roughly 2.3 million people in mountain areas as intended beneficiaries. The four lakes were flagged in a 2020 ICIMOD-UNDP report that identified 47 high-risk lakes across Nepal, Tibet and India, 21 of them in Nepal. Scientists who have studied these lakes argue the work as designed will not make downstream settlements safe: the planned drawdown is 3 metres, modelled on the earlier projects at Tsho Rolpa in 2000 and Imja in 2016. Researcher Nitesh Khadka said: “Spending millions to lower a lake by two or four metres achieves very little.” Specialists recommend at least 10 to 20 metres, and stress that the single most important element is an early warning system backed by field observation. As of The Kathmandu Post's September 6 report, construction had not begun; 2026 is being spent appointing consultants and conducting environmental assessments, with refined designs still pending.

Where the money comes from

Nepal's finance minister told Reuters that reconstruction will require roughly $4 billion to $5 billion. The National Planning Commission has begun a Post-Disaster Needs Assessment, with a report expected within five weeks to serve as the basis for a donor conference. Kathmandu plans to apply to the global Loss and Damage Fund on the grounds that climate change worsened the flood's severity. Bloomberg and The Japan Times note that this will be a live test of that new mechanism — the fund is underfunded and untested, and Nepal is one of Asia's poorest countries. The Kathmandu Post reported on September 8 that Nepal wants grants, but that loans may be unavoidable.

Anak Krakatau Erupts, Sending Ash to About 14.6 km; 108 Flights at Bali's Airport Cancelled or Rescheduled

The Bali Times · Business Today · Wego · The Traveler · 2026-09-07

Anak Krakatau, in the Sunda Strait between Java and Sumatra, erupted after activity intensified late on September 4, accompanied by roaring sounds, vibrations and lava fountains; satellite data showed volcanic ash rising to about 14.6 kilometres above sea level. The eruptions of September 4 and 5 closed Jakarta's airports until 8:59 a.m. on September 7. At Bali's I Gusti Ngurah Rai International Airport, 108 flights were cancelled or rescheduled between 7 a.m. on September 6 and 11 a.m. on September 7, mostly on Jakarta and Bandung routes; the airport itself stayed open, with domestic and international operations and passenger services continuing normally. Anak Krakatau lies roughly 1,200 kilometres west of Bali, which sits outside the ash zone; the disruption reached it because a large share of domestic traffic into Bali connects through Jakarta.
