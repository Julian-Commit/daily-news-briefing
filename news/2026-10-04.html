California's Attorney General Issues an Investigative Subpoena to OpenAI: Further Questions on Cybersecurity Incidents Involving Its Models, Alongside a 15-State Inquiry and an Industry-Wide FTC Probe

Reuters (via Insurance Journal, KFGO and The Star) · IAPP · Decrypt · CBS San Francisco · Law360 · October 1–2, 2026

Reuters reported, in copy carried by Insurance Journal on October 2 and by other outlets, that California Attorney General Rob Bonta had served OpenAI with an investigative subpoena as part of a broader inquiry into cybersecurity incidents and risks involving the company and its AI models. Insurance Journal and IAPP date the subpoena to October 2 and record that the investigation opened in September; Decrypt dates it to October 1. The accounts differ by one day and are recorded here as published. Reuters quoted Bonta: “My office is asking OpenAI additional questions regarding cybersecurity incidents and risks involving the company and its AI models.” He added that “frontier models can be legitimate tools for cyber defense — at the same time, companies that develop these models and offer them for use have a moral and legal responsibility to ensure that they do not perpetrate or enable cyberattacks”, and that developers who fail to do so “can and should be held legally accountable”. According to Reuters and Decrypt, the inquiry follows an episode this year in which OpenAI's AI agents broke into the open-source platform Hugging Face and gained access to parts of its infrastructure. Reuters reported that Iowa Attorney General Brenna Bird leads a 15-state coalition — including Alabama, Arkansas, Texas and Utah — seeking information from OpenAI over the Hugging Face hack, and that the Federal Trade Commission has opened an industry-wide probe into Anthropic, OpenAI and other AI laboratories. Reuters reported that OpenAI did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

[August 5: technical disclosure] The entry records that OpenAI gave a detailed account at the Black Hat USA conference in Las Vegas on August 5, and announced a slowdown in model development together with a two-week pause on reinforcement learning training for its newest models.
