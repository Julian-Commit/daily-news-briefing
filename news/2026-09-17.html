WMO: 2025 Was One of the Driest Years for River Discharge in 35 Years, and Glaciers Lost Around 1,400 Gigatonnes Over Three Years

WMO release · UN News · CNN · RTÉ · phys.org · 2026-09-17

According to the World Meteorological Organization's release of September 17, the agency published its State of Global Water Resources Report 2025 that day. On river discharge, the report states that 2025 was one of the driest years for river flows globally in 35 years, with below-normal discharge across 36 per cent of the global basin area, and that for the seventh consecutive year the share of river basins in "normal" conditions (34 to 38 per cent) remained below the 1991-2020 historical average of 46 per cent. On glaciers, the report describes a fourth consecutive year of widespread net ice loss across all glaciated regions: in the 2025 hydrological year alone glaciers lost about 408 gigatonnes of ice, contributing roughly 1.1 millimetres to global sea-level rise; cumulative losses from 2023 to 2025 came to around 1,400 gigatonnes, which the WMO likened to filling about 560 million Olympic-sized swimming pools. On land water, the report says global terrestrial water storage has been declining since 2014-2016, and that in 2025 nearly two-thirds of monitored wells showed conditions outside historical norms. WMO Secretary-General Celeste Saulo said in the release: "We are depleting that savings account. Global terrestrial water storage has shown a declining trend since 2014-2016." UN News and CNN reported the report's overall assessment that the water cycle has become more erratic and less predictable, swinging between too little and too much. Ireland's RTÉ summarised the findings as the world's rivers experiencing their driest year in decades. World Weather Attribution: The August 26 Nepal-Tibet Flood Was a "Compound Crisis," and Human-Caused Warming Weakened the Slope That Collapsed

Al Jazeera · AP (via Click2Houston) · CNN · WWA report · 2026-09-17

Al Jazeera and the Associated Press, via Click2Houston, reported on September 17 that World Weather Attribution (WWA) had published its analysis of the August 26 Nepal-Tibet flood. The central finding is that the disaster was not caused by a single extreme weather event but was a "compound crisis" shaped by long-term warming, glacier retreat, permafrost thaw and geological instability. According to Al Jazeera, the report states that "relentless warming has lifted the altitude of the freezing threshold," exposing ice and permafrost to damaging thaw cycles, and sets out the local conditions: July and August 2026 were the warmest months on local record, running about 5C above normal, while heavy snowfall in late 2025 added to meltwater. One Report, Two Subjects: What the Fact-Finding Mission on Iran Found Against the United States, and What It Found Against Iranian Authorities

Sources: NPR (two pieces) · ABC News (Australia) · The National · Reuters · TimesLIVE · published material from the UN Human Rights Council and the mission

What kind of document this is

The document submitted to the UN Human Rights Council on September 17 comes from the Independent International Fact-Finding Mission on Iran. Its nature matters first: this is an investigative mechanism of independent experts created by the Council in 2022, currently chaired by the Bangladeshi jurist Sara Hossain. It is not a court, and the legal threshold for its conclusions is "reasonable grounds to believe," not the "beyond reasonable doubt" standard of a criminal trial. The report therefore establishes no individual criminal liability and initiates no prosecution. This publication stresses the point because "found war crimes" reads easily as a verdict, and it is not one.

On the United States: two strikes, one charge

According to NPR and Australia's ABC, the mission's findings on the US centre on two strikes at the start of the US-Israeli war against Iran in February, under the charge of launching indiscriminate attacks resulting in loss of civilian life or injury, or damage to civilian objects. The first involved Tomahawk cruise missiles hitting the Shajareh Tayyebeh primary school in Minab; the second involved Precision Strike Missiles dispersing tungsten pellets over a sports complex and residential area in the southern city of Lamerd. In both cases the mission used the phrase "clearly identifiable" — wording that, in international humanitarian law, goes to whether an attacker could have distinguished military objectives from civilian objects. Per ABC, the mission found the US had acted "recklessly," relying on unverified intelligence about a military presence at the sites.

Why the numbers differ

Three figures need to sit side by side rather than be merged: the mission says the two strikes killed at least 178 civilians; ABC reported more than 150 killed at the Minab school, about 120 of them schoolchildren, and 22 civilians killed and injured at Lamerd; Iranian state media had earlier given 168 dead at Minab. The three come from three different counting bodies at three different moments. This publication does not reconcile them, does not take a midpoint, and does not judge which is closest to the facts. We have not obtained the full report and cannot verify the mission's counting method.

On Iranian authorities: the other half

The report is often summarised as a US war-crimes report, but its other half addresses the Iranian government. According to ABC and The National, the mission found that Iranian authorities committed crimes against humanity in the course of suppressing protests, listing unlawful killings, torture, arbitrary detention, enforced disappearances and severe restrictions on freedom of expression, and saying killings occurred on a "staggering scale." It is worth noting that the mission's original 2022 mandate concerned precisely the crackdown on protests inside Iran; the conduct of US forces entered its remit only after the 2026 war.

The two responses

Per ABC, US officials have previously stated that their military operations comply with the law of armed conflict, and a Pentagon investigation into the two strikes has not been published. Iran rejected allegations of systematic violations. As of the time of this report, we have seen no formal point-by-point response to this report from the US State Department or Defense Department, nor a full written rebuttal from Iran's permanent mission in Geneva; if either appears, the original text should govern.

Timeline

2022: the Human Rights Council creates the Independent International Fact-Finding Mission on Iran, prompted by protests and the crackdown inside Iran. February 2026: the US and Israel go to war with Iran; per ABC, at least one US missile hits the school in Minab at the war's outset. 一天里出现的四组数字

9月17日，两份文件同日发布：世界气象组织的《2025年全球水资源状况》报告，与世界天气归因组织对8月26日尼泊尔—西藏洪灾的归因分析。把它们与前一天公布的一项冰盖测量研究、以及9月上旬的一份月度气候公报放在一起，可以得到四组彼此独立的数字：河川流量、陆地储水、冰川、冰盖。它们来自不同的观测系统与不同的统计口径，本报逐组标注来源，不把它们合并成一个结论。

第一组：河川流量

据世界气象组织的发布，2025年是35年来全球河川流量最干旱的年份之一，全球流域面积的36%出现低于常年的流量。另一个可能更能说明趋势的数字是「常年状态」的占比：报告称这已是连续第七年，处于常年状态的流域占比（34%至38%）低于1991至2020年的历史平均值46%。需要注意的是，「低于常年」是相对于某一基准期的统计表述，而非绝对水量；报告的基准期为1991至2020年。爱尔兰广播电视台把这一部分概括为「全球河流经历数十年来最干旱的一年」。

第二组：陆地储水与地下水
