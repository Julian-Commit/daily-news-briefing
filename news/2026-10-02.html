NVIDIA Releases an Open Agent Safety Platform: An OpenShell Runtime Plus a Sentry Monitor on BlueField-4, With More Than 100 Participating Organisations Including Anthropic, Microsoft and JPMorganChase

NVIDIA press release · CNBC · Tom's Hardware · CSO Online · GlobeNewswire · September 28 – October 1, 2026

According to NVIDIA's press release of September 28, issued the same day via GlobeNewswire, the company launched the Open Agent Safety Platform, described as an open software platform and reference system design covering AI agent security from testing through deployment. The release and CNBC's September 28 report identify two named components: NVIDIA OpenShell, an open-source secure runtime that establishes boundaries for agents running on CPUs, traces all of their actions and enforces policy; and NVIDIA Sentry, an out-of-band watchdog running on NVIDIA BlueField-4 DPUs that continuously monitors agent behaviour and can quarantine an agent attempting to breach policy within milliseconds. The release states that OpenShell pairs with NVIDIA's Vera CPU, purpose-built for agentic AI, and that as open-source software it can be extended to third-party compute platforms including those from Arm and Intel; the software is available through NVIDIA developer resources and GitHub. CNBC and Tom's Hardware reported that the platform addresses agents escaping their sandboxes, executing unauthorised code, gaining access to critical infrastructure or bypassing guardrails, with the security boundary placed outside the models' application layer. The release records more than 100 participating organisations across infrastructure, robotics, financial services and energy, publicly naming Anthropic, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Dell Technologies, Figure, HPE, Hugging Face, JPMorganChase, Microsoft, Palantir, Palo Alto Networks, Perplexity, Red Hat, Salesforce, SAP, Scale AI, ServiceNow and SpaceXAI. In the release, NVIDIA chief executive Jensen Huang said “Safety and security require full-stack engineering”; Anthropic chief commercial officer Paul Smith said “Companies are giving AI agents more of their most important work”; and SpaceXAI president Mike Nicolls said “Safety should be enforced outside the model”. TechCrunch reported on September 29 that OpenAI is not a public supporter of the platform but is working with NVIDIA on agent security privately, including on OpenShell.

OpenAI 披露并阻断一场「对抗性蒸馏」活动：7月24日至25日峰值达1.6万次请求、涉逾4000个账户，指向与月之暗面有关联的个人

OpenAI 官方公告 · The Hacker News · Outlook India · 2026-10-01

据 OpenAI 10月1日发布的官方公告（经 The Hacker News 同日转述），该公司识别并中止了一场有组织的活动，其目的是从 OpenAI 的模型中提取受保护的推理过程。据 OpenAI 的表述，相关行为符合「对抗性蒸馏」（adversarial distillation）的特征，即系统性地、未经授权地使用一个模型的输出或推理，以帮助训练、复制或改进另一个模型；所谓受保护的推理，是模型处理任务时的内部记录，提取它可能暴露未写入最终回答的信息，并帮助他方复现模型能力。据 The Hacker News 的记录，该活动最早可观察到的行为始于2026年7月1日，7月24日至25日出现峰值，当时有来自逾4000个用户的约1.6万次尝试性请求；相关提示词模式的活动涉及超过1.5万名用户，整场活动于7月28日被完全阻断。据 The Hacker News，OpenAI 将其中一个「核心集群」与若干同北京的月之暗面（Moonshot AI）有关联的个人联系起来，并表示出于安全考虑未提供支持这一判断的技术证据。据 OpenAI，所采取的措施包括封禁从事该活动的欺诈账户、关闭一条使已持有他人加密推理内容者得以重放并还原其内容的路径，以及增加检测与暂留可能暴露推理的流式输出的检查。据 OpenAI 的公告，其给出的理由是「对抗性蒸馏构成安全与国家安全风险」，因为「被提取的推理可能被用于训练另一个模型，而不保留施加于原模型面向用户输出的那些防护措施」。该事件涉及的是通过操纵模型交互实现的提取，而非对系统的直接入侵；相关归因由 OpenAI 单方面作出，月之暗面的回应未见于上述报道。

OpenAI Discloses and Disrupts an “Adversarial Distillation” Campaign: 16,000 Requests From More Than 4,000 Accounts at Its July 24–25 Peak, Attributed to Individuals Linked to Moonshot AI

OpenAI announcement · The Hacker News · Outlook India · October 1, 2026

According to OpenAI's announcement of October 1, relayed the same day by The Hacker News, the company identified and disrupted a coordinated campaign designed to extract protected reasoning from its models. In OpenAI's wording, the activity was consistent with “adversarial distillation” — the systematic and unauthorised use of one model's outputs or reasoning to help train, reproduce or improve another model; protected reasoning is the model's internal record of working through a task, and extracting it can reveal information withheld from the final answer and help others reproduce the model's capabilities. The Hacker News recorded that the earliest observed activity began on July 1, 2026, that the campaign peaked on July 24–25 with roughly 16,000 attempted requests from more than 4,000 users, that related prompt-pattern activity spanned more than 15,000 users, and that the campaign was fully disrupted on July 28. According to The Hacker News, OpenAI linked a “core cluster” to individuals associated with Beijing-based Moonshot AI, while stating that it was not providing the technical evidence underlying that assessment for security reasons. OpenAI said its measures included banning the fraudulent accounts involved, closing a pathway that allowed someone already holding another user's encrypted reasoning to replay it and recover its contents, and adding checks to detect and hold streamed output that might expose reasoning. In its announcement, OpenAI's stated rationale was that “adversarial distillation poses safety and national security risks” because “extracted reasoning could be used to train another model without preserving the safeguards applied to the original model's user-facing outputs”. The extraction described took place through manipulated model interactions rather than a direct breach of systems; the attribution was made by OpenAI alone, and no response from Moonshot AI appears in the reporting cited here.
