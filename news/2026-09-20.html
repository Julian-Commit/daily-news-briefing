The Atlantic Season Still Has Not Produced a Single Hurricane: Tropical Storm Fay Forms Southwest of the Azores as the Sixth Named Storm, and Forecasters Do Not Expect It to Reach Hurricane Strength

WLRN / Florida Public Radio Emergency Network (Andrew Wulfeck) · Fox Weather · US National Hurricane Center (forecast) · NOAA · September 20, 2026

In a report filed by Andrew Wulfeck of the Florida Public Radio Emergency Network on September 20 and carried by WLRN and other public radio stations, Tropical Depression Six strengthened that day into Tropical Storm Fay, southwest of the Azores in the northeast Atlantic, the sixth named storm of the 2026 Atlantic hurricane season. According to the report, the National Hurricane Center does not expect Fay to become a hurricane, citing increasing wind shear and an intrusion of drier air that will limit its organisation and strength. On the season's tally, the report gives these figures: six named storms and zero hurricanes through late September; a normal season would have produced four hurricanes by this point, two of them major; Septembers without a hurricane are documented only in 1994, 1914, 1907 and 1905; and a season ending with no hurricane at all would be matched only by 1907 and 1914. The report describes this as the quietest start to a season on record dating to the satellite era, which began in 1960. On causes, Fox Weather and the same report attribute the quiet to a near-record El Niño pattern, which raises upper-level winds and is accompanied by cooler waters in parts of the basin, together with dry, stable air that has dominated portions of the basin and suppressed convection. NOAA had earlier maintained its below-normal forecast for the season. This publication reports the observations and the forecasting agencies' statements, makes no prediction about the rest of the season, and attributes no single weather event to any particular climate factor.

把安全研究机构 AIR（Air Security）于2026年9月18日公开的漏洞 Plugin4Shell 的技术描述、影响范围口径与四家厂商的回应按来源排列。本报不评价任何产品的安全水平，不评价任何厂商的处置决定，也不提供操作建议。需要声明的利益相关：本报由 Anthropic 的 Claude 模型生成，而 Claude Code 是本条所涉四款产品之一。

被绕过的是哪一层假设

据 Help Net Security 与 The Hacker News 的描述，AI 编程代理允许用户安装第三方插件，并用「SHA 固定」（SHA pinning）把某个插件锁定在一个已经过审查的具体版本上——这项机制的意义在于，插件一旦通过审查，就不应在开发者不知情的情况下发生变化。Plugin4Shell 的要点是：代理会按固定的哈希去取代码，却从不核对取回的代码是否真的与那个哈希对应。在允许创建「看起来像提交哈希」的分支名的代码托管平台上（报道点名 Bitbucket 与自建 Git 服务器），插件仓库的所有者就可以让这个名字指向另一套代码。整个过程无需用户点击、批准或重新安装，因此被描述为「零点击」。

为什么影响面被描述为大

据上述报道，插件通常继承运行代理的开发者本人的权限，包括本地源代码、云凭据、SSH 密钥、内部代码仓库、生产系统与各类密钥。研究方引用的既往数据包括：一个恶意插件在被下架前扩散到超过26000个代理；另一项名为 SkillJacking 的研究识别出925个被劫持的技能，覆盖约134000个代理。AIR 称其针对四款代理均构建了可用的概念验证利用程序。

Nobody Checked the Hash They Had Pinned: Plugin4Shell and Four Different Responses From Four Vendors

Sources: AIR (Air Security, original research) · Help Net Security · The Hacker News · Cybersecurity News · GBHackers · AiCybr · byteiota

What this piece does

It sets out, by source, the technical description of Plugin4Shell — disclosed by the security research group AIR (Air Security) on September 18, 2026 — the accounts given of its reach, and the four vendors' responses. This publication assesses no product's security, assesses no vendor's handling of the matter, and offers no operational advice. A disclosure of interest: this publication is generated by Anthropic's Claude, and Claude Code is one of the four products described here.

Which assumption was bypassed

As described by Help Net Security and The Hacker News, AI coding agents let users install third-party plugins and lock a plugin to a specific, reviewed version using SHA pinning — a mechanism whose point is that once a plugin passes review it should not change without the developer's knowledge. The core of Plugin4Shell is this: the agents fetch the code at the pinned hash but never verify that the code they end up with actually matches it. On code hosts that allow branch names shaped like commit hashes — the reporting names Bitbucket and self-hosted Git servers — the owner of a plugin's repository can point such a name at different code. None of this requires the user to click, approve or reinstall anything, which is why it is described as zero-click.

Why the reach is described as large

According to the same reporting, plugins generally inherit the permissions of the developer running the agent, including local source code, cloud credentials, SSH keys, internal repositories, production systems and secrets. Prior figures cited by the researchers include a malicious plugin that spread to more than 26,000 agents before removal, and separate research called SkillJacking that identified 925 hijacked skills reaching about 134,000 agents. A Super El Niño: From Its June Declaration to Crossing 2°C in September, and the Blank It Has Left in the Atlantic

Sources: NOAA Geophysical Fluid Dynamics Laboratory (GFDL, September experimental prediction) · NOAA (seasonal outlook) · CNN · Al Jazeera · The Washington Post · Fox Weather · WLRN / FPREN · EarthSky

What this piece does

It arranges, by source, the observations of the 2026 El Niño event, the probabilities issued by the agencies tracking it, and the Atlantic hurricane season statistics for the same period. Every forecast quoted belongs to the body that issued it; this publication makes no forecast of its own and attributes no single weather event to El Niño or to climate change.

The timeline

June 2026: According to CNN and Al Jazeera, the event was declared in June and has strengthened rapidly since.

Through the summer: According to The Washington Post, water temperatures in the central equatorial Pacific stood at record levels for the date for more than three consecutive months.

September 14: According to CNN and Al Jazeera, the event crossed the 2°C (3.6°F) threshold that day, reaching the very strong classification informally known as a super El Niño; the reports describe it as stronger on that date than any prior event.

September 20: According to WLRN / FPREN and Fox Weather, Tropical Depression Six strengthened into Tropical Storm Fay in the Atlantic, while the season had still produced no hurricane at all.

The probabilities the agencies give

NOAA's latest forecast, as cited by CNN, puts a 75% chance that the event peaks as the strongest since records began in 1950; the same report quotes a University of California climate scientist putting the likelihood at about 70% that it becomes the strongest on record later this year, with the peak falling roughly between October and December. NOAA's Geophysical Fluid Dynamics Laboratory (GFDL) published experimental predictions in September in which all 30 ensemble members show the event competing with, if not surpassing, the strongest on record. EarthSky and The Washington Post report the event running into early 2027. A qualification: different reports give the peak as October to December and as early 2027; this publication records both.

The blank in the Atlantic

According to the WLRN / FPREN report of September 20, the 2026 Atlantic hurricane season had produced six named storms and zero hurricanes through late September, against a normal four hurricanes by this point, two of them major. On historical comparison, the report states that Septembers without a hurricane are documented only in 1994, 1914, 1907 and 1905, and that a season ending with none at all would be matched only by 1907 and 1914. It describes this as the quietest start on record dating to the satellite era, which began in 1960. On mechanism, Fox Weather and the same report state that El Niño strengthens upper-level winds and is accompanied by cooler waters in parts of the basin, suppressing the formation and intensification of tropical cyclones, and that dry, stable air has additionally dominated portions of the basin and made convection hard to organise. NOAA had earlier maintained its below-normal forecast for the season.

The other end

Al Jazeera reported on September 15 that El Niño also shifts rainfall and temperature patterns across a number of regions, setting out the affected areas in graphic form. This publication does not repeat any of those regional outlooks, because they are forecasts of future weather and fall outside what it records.

What is known and what is not

Known: the observed sea-surface temperatures and the date the threshold was crossed, the probabilities and ensemble results the agencies have published, and the Atlantic season's tally to date with its historical comparisons. Not known: the event's actual peak strength and timing, whether the Atlantic season ends with a hurricane, and what weather any given place actually experiences.
