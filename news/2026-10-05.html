The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine Goes to Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel “For Their Discoveries Concerning Light-Gated Ion Channels and Optogenetics”, With 12 Million Swedish Kronor Shared Equally

NobelPrize.org · STAT News · Scientific American · Euronews · Chemical & Engineering News · CNN · October 5, 2026

NobelPrize.org and STAT News reported on October 5 that the Nobel Assembly at the Karolinska Institutet had decided to award the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine jointly to Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel “for their discoveries concerning light-gated ion channels and optogenetics”. STAT News recorded that the Nobel Assembly's secretary-general, Thomas Perlmann, made the announcement, and that the three will share 12 million Swedish kronor equally — converted by STAT News to about $1.2 million and by Euronews to about €1.09 million. According to STAT News, Deisseroth, 55, is at Stanford University and the Howard Hughes Medical Institute; Hegemann, 72, is at the Humboldt University of Berlin; and Nagel, 73, is at the University of Würzburg in Germany. Scientific American reported that the work of Hegemann and Nagel established rhodopsin proteins as central to the light response of the alga Chlamydomonas, and that Deisseroth then applied those findings to neurons in mammals. STAT News quoted Deisseroth saying that optogenetics uses light “to turn cells on or off with that millisecond precision and the cellular resolution that is essential to brain function”, and that “what we're doing now is leveraging this precision to find new treatments, new medications”. Euronews quoted the chair of the Nobel Committee, Per Svenningsson: “This method is now being used in laboratories around the world to reveal the brain's mysteries.” CNN quoted Abdel El Manira of the Karolinska Institutet saying that “optogenetics has given us a powerful means to take on these challenges”. Euronews reported that the remaining 2026 prizes follow on Tuesday (physics), Wednesday (chemistry), Thursday (literature), Friday (peace) and October 12 (economic sciences), with the ceremony on December 10.

An announcement published by the European Commission's Directorate-General for Research and Innovation on October 2, and a Cyprus Mail report the same day, recorded the opening of a €230 million Horizon Europe research and innovation call on nature, supporting the protection, restoration and mainstreaming of biodiversity. According to Innovation News Network, the call covers ten nature research topics spanning the biodiversity impacts of chemicals and industry, ecosystem restoration, wildlife trafficking, monitoring and ocean observation; named topics include understanding the nature and extent of the effects of PFAS on biodiversity and ecosystem services, living labs co-creating solutions for the restoration of ecosystems to address implementation bottlenecks and improve resilience to risks, and systemic approaches to accelerate transformative change for biodiversity.
