The US, Denmark and Greenland Sign an Arctic Security Agreement on the UNGA Margins: Pituffik Expanded, Two New Bases, No Expiration Date

Al Jazeera · CBS News · ABC News · The Hill · Fox News · September 22, 2026

Al Jazeera, CBS News and ABC News reported on September 22 that US President Donald Trump, Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen and Greenland Premier Jens-Frederik Nielsen signed a trilateral security agreement that day on the margins of the UN General Assembly in New York. On the terms, according to those reports: the United States may modernise and expand Pituffik Space Base in north-western Greenland and establish further bases at Narsarsuaq in the south and Mestersvig in the east, which CBS News reported are roughly 950 miles apart; US aircraft may fly over and land anywhere in Greenland, including over its territorial waters; no non-NATO country may establish military installations on the island without the agreement of all three parties; and screening is tightened on investment from non-NATO and non-EU countries in sensitive sectors including telecommunications, undersea cables, ports and critical minerals. The agreement carries no expiration date and persists if Greenland becomes independent. ABC News reported that the deal falls short of Trump’s earlier calls for “complete and total control”: expansion beyond the pre-agreed locations is subject to a 90-day review, escalating to deputy and minister level if no consensus is reached. Fox News reported that the United States makes no payment to Denmark or Greenland. On the statements: Trump said the deal is “tremendous for Europe and for the United States and for everybody”; Frederiksen called it “a deal that can last forever” and said Denmark is “not giving up sovereignty”; Nielsen said the agreement “should put to rest any doubt today and in the future about where Greenland’s loyalty lies.” Al Jazeera reported that NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte and European Council President Antonio Costa both welcomed the agreement, with Costa calling it “a positive step for transatlantic cooperation and Arctic security.” This briefing records the main terms and the signatories’ statements only, and does not evaluate any country’s sovereignty or defence policy.

According to the University of Miami Rosenstiel School news release re-run by ScienceDaily on September 22, a study based on direct measurements finds that the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) has weakened continuously for nearly two decades. The paper appeared in Science Advances on April 8, 2026 (Volume 12, Issue 15), DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adz7738, authored by Qianjiang Xing, Shane Elipot, William E. Johns, David A. Smeed, Ben I. Moat and John W. Loder. On method and data, per the release and phys.org: the study analysed water temperature, salinity and current velocity recorded since 2004 by four mooring arrays along the western boundary of the North Atlantic, with instruments anchored to the seafloor below roughly 1,000 metres, spanning latitudes from about 16.5°N to 42.5°N; at 26°N the rate of decline over 2004–2023 was 2.6 ± 0.7 Sv per decade, corresponding to a fall from about 20 Sv to about 15 Sv. The release quotes co-author Shane Elipot as saying that “a weaker AMOC can shift weather patterns, potentially leading to more extreme storms, changes in rainfall, or colder winters in some regions.” To be clear: this is a peer-reviewed paper published in April, recorded here because it was re-run on September 22; this briefing does not forecast the future behaviour of the circulation.
