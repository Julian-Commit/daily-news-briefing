Two of the world's shipping chokepoints were in focus on September 10. Yemen's Houthis seized the Red Sea port city of Mocha and Zuqar Island and pushed toward the Bab el-Mandeb strait; at the other end of the Arabian Peninsula, the exchange of attacks between the United States and Iran around the Strait of Hormuz continued, and Brent crude settled at $101.21 a barrel on September 9, its highest since May 22. On September 9 the board of the International Atomic Energy Agency voted 23 in favour, three against and eight abstaining to report Iran to the UN Security Council, the first such referral in 20 years. In technology, Google said it would invest at least €13 billion in AI infrastructure in Finland over two years and signed a long-term power purchase agreement covering the Loviisa nuclear plant, its first nuclear deal outside the United States. On climate, the Copernicus Climate Change Service published its monthly bulletin on September 10: the global average surface air temperature in August 2026 was 16.96°C, tying July 2023 as the warmest month on record and standing 1.65°C above the pre-industrial level.

Houthis Seize the Red Sea Port of Mocha and Zuqar Island, Pushing Toward Bab el-Mandeb; More Than 500 Killed in a Week of Fighting

Euronews (AFP) · Reuters · South China Morning Post · The Media Line · The New Arab · 2026-09-10

Yemen's Houthis captured the Red Sea port city of Mocha on September 10 and advanced toward the Bab el-Mandeb strait. According to a Yemeni military source, the group also took Zuqar Island after “rocket attacks and a ground assault carried out using boats transporting fighters”; witnesses said they saw Houthi fighters entering the city chanting the movement's political slogan. Reuters, citing three Yemeni government sources, reported that Houthi forces had entered Mocha and taken control of it, and that government forces and their allies were relocating south to Dhubab, which sits directly on the strait across from Perim Island. Mocha had been held by Yemen's internationally recognised government and had been under sustained attack since early August, when more than 25 missiles struck it within days and port operations halted. The city lies less than 50 miles north of Bab el-Mandeb. Unconfirmed reports said Houthi forces landed on Perim Island by the evening of September 10. Major-General Sadeq Duwaid of the Yemeni government forces said: “The Houthi militia seeks to establish a presence along the shores of the Bab al-Mandeb Strait and transform the area into a situation resembling that of the Strait of Hormuz.” On the scale of the fighting, the Houthis launched a large-scale offensive last week against Saudi-backed government forces, and Saudi forces responded with roughly 40 airstrikes across several governorates; an AFP tally puts the death toll since last week at more than 500, mostly combatants. Hundreds of women and children left Mocha on September 10 for the government-held provinces of Lahj and Aden. Bab el-Mandeb links Asia to Europe via the Red Sea and the Suez Canal; together with the Strait of Hormuz it carries a quarter of daily petroleum shipments and close to a third of global maritime trade. Yemen's civil war returned to open hostilities in July, when the Houthis allowed an Iranian aircraft to land in defiance of Saudi control of Yemeni airspace and Riyadh retaliated by striking the airport. 国际原子能机构理事会以23票赞成、3票反对、8票弃权，20年来首次将伊朗提交联合国安理会

France 24 (AFP) · AP News · The Globe and Mail · ABC News · The Jerusalem Post · 2026-09-09

国际原子能机构（IAEA）由35国组成的理事会9月9日通过决议，就核不遵约问题将伊朗提交联合国安理会，表决结果为23票赞成、3票反对、8票弃权，投反对票的是俄罗斯、中国与尼日尔。决议由英国、法国、德国与美国共同提交，要求「机构总干事向联合国安全理事会与联合国大会报告」伊朗问题。这是20年来伊朗首次因未履行核报告义务被提交安理会。触发事由是伊朗在一项长期调查中未予配合——调查涉及监察员在伊朗多个未申报地点检测到的铀痕迹。背景方面，自2025年6月以色列与美国对伊朗核设施发动打击以来，IAEA一直被拒绝进入伊朗的关键核设施；另有设施在2026年2月28日爆发的冲突中受损。伊朗常驻IAEA代表礼萨·纳贾菲（Reza Najafi）称，该决议「是在美国及其盟友的政治压力下通过的」，并称其「完全适得其反」。分析人士阿里·瓦埃兹（Ali Vaez）就后续影响表示：「归根结底，这是一次徒劳的操作。」多家报道指出，鉴于俄罗斯与中国在安理会拥有否决权、可以阻止针对德黑兰的制裁，此举在实际效果上主要具有象征意义。交叉核验：France 24 (AFP) · AP News · The Globe and Mail · ABC News · The Jerusalem Post · Military.com

IAEA Board Reports Iran to the UN Security Council by 23 Votes to 3 With 8 Abstentions, the First Referral in 20 Years

France 24 (AFP) · AP News · The Globe and Mail · ABC News · The Jerusalem Post · 2026-09-09

The 35-member board of governors of the International Atomic Energy Agency voted on September 9 to refer Iran to the UN Security Council over nuclear non-compliance. The resolution passed with 23 votes in favour, three against and eight abstentions; Russia, China and Niger voted against. It was submitted by Britain, France, Germany and the United States, and requests that the agency's director general report Iran to both the Security Council and the UN General Assembly. It is the first time in 20 years that Iran has been referred to the Council for failing to meet its nuclear reporting obligations. The trigger was Iran's failure to cooperate with a long-running investigation into uranium traces detected by inspectors at several undeclared sites in the country. On background, the IAEA has been denied access to key Iranian nuclear facilities since June 2025, when Israel and the United States struck nuclear sites; further facilities were damaged in the conflict that erupted on February 28, 2026. Iran's representative to the IAEA, Reza Najafi, said the resolution “was passed under political pressure from the US and its allies” and called it “completely counterproductive.” On its likely effect, the analyst Ali Vaez said: “Ultimately, though, it is an exercise in futility.” Multiple outlets noted that because Russia and China hold vetoes on the Security Council and can block sanctions against Tehran, the move is largely symbolic in practical terms. Google to Invest at Least €13 Billion in Finnish AI Infrastructure Over Two Years and Buy Up to Half the Output of the Loviisa Nuclear Plant

Reuters · Bloomberg · Business Recorder · Tech Times · Arkansas Democrat-Gazette · 2026-09-10

Alphabet's Google said it will invest at least €13 billion (about $15.1 billion) in AI infrastructure in Finland over the next two years, its largest European investment. The plan includes three new data centres in the north of the country, where winter temperatures can fall well below minus 10°C. The accompanying power arrangement is a long-term purchase agreement with the Nordic utility Fortum Oyj under which Google will buy as much as 50% of the capacity of the Loviisa nuclear plant, allowing it to run to the end of its operating licence in 2050. Bloomberg describes the agreement as running 20 years, while Business Recorder (citing Reuters) and Tech Times put it at 22; both figures are given here. Bloomberg also reported that it is the first agreement of its kind in Europe and will help unlock roughly €1 billion of investment needed to keep the plant running for decades. Ruth Porat, Google's president and chief investment officer, said: “We call this BYOP, bring your own power. This is Google's first nuclear energy deal outside of the United States.” On background, Alphabet has raised its global annual investment to $195–205 billion to meet expanding AI computing demand, and reporting notes that the Nordic region has attracted companies including Microsoft and ByteDance because its cold climate aids cooling and its grids offer access to renewable power. Copernicus: August 2026 Averaged 16.96°C Globally, Tying July 2023 as the Hottest Month on Record at 1.65°C Above Pre-Industrial

Copernicus C3S · Euronews · Travel Tomorrow · Sunday Guardian · 2026-09-10

The EU's Copernicus Climate Change Service published its monthly climate bulletin on September 10. The global average surface air temperature in August 2026 was 16.96°C, 0.85°C above the 1991–2020 average for the month and 1.65°C above the 1850–1900 pre-industrial baseline, tying July 2023 as the warmest month on record in the ERA5 dataset. It was also the first month to exceed the 1.5°C threshold since November 2025. At sea, the monthly average sea surface temperature over the extra-polar ocean between 60°S and 60°N was 21.07°C — the highest on record for August and the joint highest for any month, tied with March 2024 — while the highest daily global extra-polar sea surface temperature reached 21.11°C. Record-high sea surface temperatures occurred in the tropical Pacific, along the Atlantic coast and in the western Mediterranean. Seasonally, June to August 2026 was the joint warmest such period on record globally, level with 2024; Western Europe recorded its warmest summer on record, surpassing 2003, while Europe as a landmass had its third warmest, behind 2024 and 2022. Arctic sea ice extent ranked 12th lowest for August and Antarctic sea ice extent ranked 4th lowest. Samantha Burgess, Strategic Lead for Climate at the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts, said: “August 2026 was a remarkable month in the global climate record … marking the return of global temperatures above 1.5°C.” Cross-verified: Copernicus C3S bulletin · Euronews · Travel Tomorrow · Sunday Guardian · YubaNet · World Today Journal

两个海峡同时收紧：从霍尔木兹到曼德的一周

综合信源：Euronews (AFP) · Reuters · France 24 (AFP) · Arab News · The Media Line · Rigzone · CNBC · South China Morning Post

时间线：9月8日至9月10日

9月8日，美国中央司令部（CENTCOM）称击毁五艘与伊朗伊斯兰革命卫队关联的原油运输船。同日，胡塞武装袭击沙特哈米斯穆谢特（Khamis Mushait）的一处空军基地以及邻近城市的石油设施，致73人受伤，为对沙特规模最大的袭击之一。9月9日，IAEA理事会以23票赞成、3票反对、8票弃权将伊朗提交联合国安理会；同日布伦特原油结算价报每桶101.21美元，为5月22日以来最高；巴基斯坦向伊朗转达沙特方面的警告。9月10日，胡塞武装夺取摩卡与祖盖尔岛，向曼德海峡推进；原油续涨。

两个咽喉的量级

Sources: Reuters · Bloomberg · Business Recorder · CNBC · TechNode · DigiTimes · Datacenterdynamics · South China Morning Post · US DOE

The power bill: from Iowa to Finland

On September 8, the US Department of Energy announced financial close on a loan of up to $1.9 billion to NextEra Energy to restart the 615 MW Duane Arnold nuclear plant in Iowa; Google has contracted for its output under a 25-year power purchase agreement to serve AI data centres, with commercial operation targeted for the first quarter of 2029. Two days later, on September 10, Google said it would invest at least €13 billion (about $15.1 billion) over two years in Finland, building three data centres in the north of the country, and signed a long-term agreement with Fortum Oyj to take up to 50% of the capacity of the Loviisa nuclear plant, keeping it running to the end of its operating licence in 2050. Outlets differ on the term: Bloomberg reports 20 years, while Business Recorder and Tech Times say 22. Ruth Porat, Google's president and chief investment officer, called the model “BYOP, bring your own power,” and said it was Google's first nuclear deal outside the United States. Bloomberg described it as the first agreement of its kind in Europe and said it would unlock roughly €1 billion of investment.

Why nuclear, and why the Nordics

Alphabet has raised its global annual investment to $195–205 billion. Reporting points to two draws for data centres in the Nordics: winter temperatures well below minus 10°C, which help with cooling, and grid access to renewable power. Microsoft and ByteDance already operate in the region. In the Loviisa case specifically, Google's long-term purchase commitment also resolves a problem for the operator — the capital needed to keep the plant running for decades.

The silicon bill: west and east

In the West, Qualcomm said on September 8 that it would work with AWS across multiple generations of custom silicon focused on inference workloads, issuing Amazon warrants for 25 million shares at $161.26 each, vesting against as much as $60 billion in purchases. In the East, JD Cloud announced on September 9 that it will build a 100,000-GPU cluster running entirely on Moore Threads chips for large-model training and inference and for embodied-AI and robotics workloads. It is the first deployment of domestic GPUs at that scale by a major Chinese cloud provider, and a tenfold scale-up of a 10,000-GPU cluster the two companies already operate together. Moore Threads was founded in 2020 by former Nvidia executive Zhang Jianzhong and listed on the Shanghai Stock Exchange's STAR Market in December 2025.

The numbers policy has put on paper

China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology published its 15th Five-Year Plan on September 7, targeting 9,800 EFLOPS of intelligent computing capacity by 2030 and 3.8 trillion yuan (about $532 billion) in cumulative information-infrastructure investment over 2026–2030. For comparison, the 2025 figure was 1,590 EFLOPS, and China's intelligent computing capacity at FP16 precision reached 2,185 EFLOPS by the end of June 2026, up 177% year on year. The plan calls for the “orderly deployment” of clusters of 10,000 cards and of 100,000 or more, and for greater effort to adapt infrastructure to home-grown chips.

What remains open

Three items lack independent third-party data: the scaling-efficiency figure Moore Threads gives for the 100,000-GPU cluster, which Tech Times noted on September 10 has not been independently audited; the pricing terms of the Loviisa agreement, which were not disclosed; and the gap between national compute targets and actual delivery. Neither Google nor Fortum disclosed the price of the power.

1.5摄氏度：8月的观测数据与联合国的「过冲」报告，同一个月里的两份文件

综合信源：Copernicus C3S · UNEP · AFP (phys.org) · Business Standard · The National · Engineering & Technology · Euronews · UN News

两份文件，相隔八天

9月2日，联合国环境规划署（UNEP）发布题为《限制过冲》（Limiting Overshoot）的报告，结论是：目前已不存在可将全球升温控制在1.5摄氏度以下的可信科学路径。9月10日，欧盟哥白尼气候变化服务（C3S）发布8月月度公报：8月全球地表平均气温较工业化前基准高1.65摄氏度，为2025年11月以来首个越过1.5摄氏度线的月份。前者是关于未来路径的评估，后者是关于单月的观测——两者是不同性质的陈述，本报分开列出。

观测端：8月的具体数字

2026年8月全球地表平均气温16.96摄氏度，较1991至2020年同期均值高0.85摄氏度，与2023年7月并列为ERA5数据集中最热月份。海洋方面，60°S至60°N极地外海域月平均海表温度21.07摄氏度，为8月最高值，并与2024年3月并列为所有月份最高；单日最高值21.11摄氏度。破纪录海温出现在热带太平洋、大西洋沿岸与地中海西部。季节尺度上，6月至8月为全球并列最热夏季（与2024年持平）；西欧为有记录以来最热夏季，超过2003年；欧洲陆地整体为第三热。北极海冰8月范围为第12低，南极为第4低。

评估端：UNEP说了什么

UNEP的结论是，即便在最好的情景下，全球升温峰值也将达到约1.8摄氏度。报告提出的路径被称为「过冲—达峰—回落」（overshoot, peak, and decline），即在承认越过1.5摄氏度已不可避免的前提下，尽可能压低过冲的幅度与持续时间，并争取尽早回到该阈值以下。报告强调，要回到阈值以下，既需要温室气体排放的快速下降，也需要对已存在于大气中的二氧化碳进行大规模移除。

两个概念需要区分

《巴黎协定》的1.5摄氏度目标指的是长期平均温升，而非单一月份或单一年份的数值。C3S报告的是2026年8月这一个月的观测值；UNEP评估的是长期路径。8月越过1.5摄氏度不等于《巴黎协定》目标已在协定定义上被突破，但C3S指出这是自2025年11月以来的首次回归。C3S将8月海温偏高归因于化石燃料燃烧带来的全球增温，以及赤道太平洋在强厄尔尼诺条件下海表温度的快速上升。

本报不做的判断

报告与观测均未给出具体年份的越线时点，本报亦不对此作推测。UNEP所述「回落」所依赖的大规模碳移除，其可行规模与成本在报告中未给出确定结论。

1.5°C: August's Observations and the UN's Overshoot Report, Two Documents in One Month

Sources: Copernicus C3S · UNEP · AFP (phys.org) · Business Standard · The National · Engineering & Technology · Euronews · UN News

Two documents, eight days apart

On September 2, the UN Environment Programme released a report titled Limiting Overshoot, concluding that there are no longer any plausible scientific pathways that keep global warming below 1.5°C. On September 10, the EU's Copernicus Climate Change Service published its August bulletin: the global average surface air temperature for the month was 1.65°C above the pre-industrial baseline, the first month above the 1.5°C line since November 2025. The first is an assessment of future pathways; the second is an observation of a single month. They are different kinds of statement, and are listed separately here.

The observations: August in figures

August 2026 averaged 16.96°C globally, 0.85°C above the 1991–2020 average for the month, tying July 2023 as the warmest month in the ERA5 dataset. At sea, the monthly average sea surface temperature over the extra-polar ocean between 60°S and 60°N was 21.07°C, the highest for any August and the joint highest for any month, tied with March 2024; the highest single-day value was 21.11°C. Record sea surface temperatures occurred in the tropical Pacific, along the Atlantic coast and in the western Mediterranean. Seasonally, June to August was the joint warmest summer globally, level with 2024; Western Europe had its warmest summer on record, surpassing 2003; and Europe as a landmass had its third warmest. Arctic sea ice extent was the 12th lowest for August, Antarctic the fourth lowest.

The assessment: what UNEP said

UNEP concluded that even under the best case, global temperatures would peak at around 1.8°C. The pathway it sets out is called “overshoot, peak, and decline”: accepting that crossing 1.5°C is now unavoidable, then minimising the magnitude and duration of the overshoot and returning below the threshold as soon as possible. The report stresses that returning below it requires both rapid reductions in greenhouse gas emissions and large-scale removal of carbon dioxide already in the atmosphere.

Two concepts that need separating

The 1.5°C goal in the Paris Agreement refers to long-term average warming, not the value for a single month or year. C3S reports an observation for the month of August 2026; UNEP assesses long-term pathways. A single month above 1.5°C does not mean the Paris target has been breached as the agreement defines it, though C3S notes it is the first such return since November 2025. C3S attributes August's elevated ocean temperatures to global heating from burning fossil fuels and to rapidly rising sea surface temperatures in the equatorial Pacific under strong El Niño conditions.

What this issue does not conclude

Neither the report nor the observations name a specific year for crossing the threshold, and this issue does not speculate on one. The feasible scale and cost of the large-scale carbon removal on which UNEP's “decline” depends are not settled in the report.
