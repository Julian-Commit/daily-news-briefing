The European Commission Proposes the EU Kids Act: No Social Media Under 13, One-Hour "Mini Accounts" for 13- to 15-Year-Olds, and AI Chatbots Off by Default

UPI · Euronews Next · Engadget · IAPP · Dataconomy · 2026-09-17

UPI reported on September 17 that the European Commission introduced that day a legislative proposal called the EU KIDS Act, announced by Commission President Ursula von der Leyen. On age thresholds, UPI, Euronews and Engadget report that the proposal sets an absolute bar for children under 13; 13- to 15-year-olds would be limited to "mini accounts" linked to a parent or guardian's main account, confined to age-appropriate social media and video-sharing platforms, capped at one hour of screen time a day and restricted in their contact with other accounts; only at 15 could a child open an account of their own. On product features, the proposal would require the removal of addictive design, listing infinite scroll, autoplay, engagement rewards, recommendation algorithms that pull minors into "rabbit holes" of harmful content, push notifications during sleeping hours, and unsolicited contact from strangers; default settings would have to be private profiles with location, camera and microphone access disabled. On AI, UPI and Euronews report that AI companion chatbots must be disabled by default and may not interact in ways that foster emotional dependency; the scope covers social media, video platforms, gaming platforms and AI chatbots. On enforcement, app stores and operating systems would have to apply age-assurance filters, online services would confirm age through an EU age-verification app, and large platforms would submit compliance plans to the Commission and to independent auditors. On process, UPI and the IAPP report that the proposal has been forwarded to the European Parliament and the Council, with the Commission saying swift adoption is essential; published reporting gives no date of entry into force. This publication notes that it has not obtained the legislative text and that the provisions above are taken from the cited reports. NPR Investigation: A Google-Led Lobbying Effort Helped Draft State Chatbot-Safety Laws in the US While Pushing for Exemptions

NPR (original investigation, syndicated by member stations including KPBS, STLPR, WYPR and WUSF) · 2026-09-18

NPR published an investigation on September 18 reporting that a technology-industry lobbying effort led by Google supplied model language to chatbot-safety bills in multiple US states while pushing to insert exemptions, and that lobbyists for Meta and Roblox also sought to add their own wording and carve-outs. On the specific provisions, the report states that Colorado House Bill 26-1263, signed by Governor Jared Polis in May 2026, excludes 12 categories of chatbot, among them products for developers or researchers, commerce and customer-service products, chatbots built into search engines, and business-productivity tools; that the phrase "a feature within another software application, web interface, or computer program" appears in at least eight states' bills; and that other language limits the laws to minor "account holders," which the report says could place products usable without registration outside their scope. The report also states that Hawaii enacted its measure as Act 248 after amendments, that bills were enacted in Idaho, Georgia, Iowa, Washington, Nebraska and Oregon, and that Arizona Governor Katie Hobbs, a Democrat, vetoed her state's bill. People named in the report include Cynthia Montoya, mother of Juliana, a teenager who died in November 2023, who testified before the Colorado Senate; Laura Marquez-Garrett, an attorney at the Social Media Victims Law Center; Hawaii state Representative Trish La Chica; and the Colorado bill's sponsor, state Representative Sean Camacho, with co-sponsor state Senator John Carson. On responses, the report quotes Google as saying it "is committed to supporting thoughtful, effective AI legislation that protects consumers while fostering innovation" and that it "actively collaborates with lawmakers, industry peers, and community advocates to help shape policy frameworks"; Healthier Colorado said HB26-1263 "is not an industry bill in disguise" and reflected "an extensive, diverse stakeholder process"; Meta and Roblox declined to comment. 谁在替未成年人和聊天机器人定规则：布鲁塞尔的一份提案，与美国各州法案里的豁免条款

综合信源：UPI · Euronews Next（两篇） · Engadget · IAPP · Dataconomy · NPR（原始调查报道，经多家公共广播成员台转载）

同一个问题，两天之内出现了两种答案

9月17日与18日，「未成年人能以何种方式使用社交平台与AI聊天机器人」这个问题在大西洋两岸分别有了新的进展。欧盟走的是一部统一法规、事前义务的路线；美国走的是逐州立法、由定义与豁免条款决定适用范围的路线。本报把两者并列，是因为它们处理的是同一个对象，而落点很不一样。

布鲁塞尔：一部法规，一套事前义务

据合众国际社（UPI）、Euronews与Engadget，欧盟委员会9月17日提出《欧盟儿童法案》（EU KIDS Act），由委员会主席冯德莱恩公布。年龄门槛为三档：13岁以下绝对禁止使用社交媒体；13至15岁只能使用与家长或监护人主账户关联的「迷你账户」，限于适龄平台、每日屏幕时间上限一小时、限制与其他账户的接触；满15岁方可自行开设账户。产品设计层面，提案要求移除无限下滑、自动播放、参与奖励机制、把未成年人拖入有害内容「兔子洞」的推荐算法、睡眠时段推送与陌生人主动联系；默认设置须为私密资料页并关闭定位、摄像头与麦克风。就AI而言，据UPI与Euronews，AI伴侣类聊天机器人须默认关闭，且不得以培养情感依赖的方式互动。执行层面，应用商店与操作系统须落实年龄核验过滤，在线服务须通过欧盟年龄核验应用确认年龄，大型平台须向委员会与独立审计方提交合规方案。覆盖对象包括社交媒体、视频平台、游戏平台与AI聊天机器人。程序上，据UPI与IAPP，提案已提交欧洲议会与欧盟理事会，公开报道未给出生效时间表。

据NPR 9月18日的调查报道，美国多州今年出现措辞相近的聊天机器人安全法案，而一场由谷歌牵头的行业游说在其中提供了条文模板并推动豁免；Meta与Roblox的游说人员亦试图加入各自的措辞。报道列举的豁免类型包括：面向开发者或研究人员的产品、商务与客户服务类产品、搜索引擎内置的聊天机器人、商务生产力工具，以及「另一软件应用、网页界面或计算机程序中的一项功能」——报道称后一表述出现在至少8个州的法案中。另有条文把适用对象限定为未成年「账户持有人」，据报道这可能使无须注册即可使用的产品不在适用范围内。立法结果方面，报道称科罗拉多州HB 26-1263于5月签署成法并排除了12类聊天机器人，夏威夷以第248号法案形式在修订后通过，爱达荷、佐治亚、艾奥瓦、华盛顿、内布拉斯加与俄勒冈亦有法案完成立法，亚利桑那州州长霍布斯否决了该州法案。

Who Writes the Rules for Minors and Chatbots: One Proposal in Brussels, and the Exemptions Inside US State Bills

Sources: UPI · Euronews Next (two pieces) · Engadget · IAPP · Dataconomy · NPR (original investigation, syndicated by public-radio member stations)

One question, two answers within two days

On September 17 and 18, the question of how minors may use social platforms and AI chatbots advanced on both sides of the Atlantic. The European Union is taking the route of a single regulation with ex-ante obligations; the United States is taking the route of state-by-state legislation in which definitions and exemptions decide who is covered. This publication sets the two side by side because they address the same object and land in very different places.

Brussels: one regulation, a set of ex-ante duties

According to UPI, Euronews and Engadget, the European Commission proposed the EU KIDS Act on September 17, announced by Commission President Ursula von der Leyen. There are three age tiers: an absolute bar on social media for under-13s; for 13- to 15-year-olds, only "mini accounts" linked to a parent or guardian's main account, confined to age-appropriate platforms, capped at one hour of screen time a day and restricted in contact with other accounts; and own accounts only from 15. On product design, the proposal would require removal of infinite scroll, autoplay, engagement rewards, recommendation algorithms that pull minors into "rabbit holes" of harmful content, notifications during sleeping hours and unsolicited contact from strangers; defaults would be private profiles with location, camera and microphone off. On AI, UPI and Euronews report that AI companion chatbots must be off by default and may not interact in ways that foster emotional dependency. On enforcement, app stores and operating systems would apply age-assurance filters, online services would confirm age through an EU age-verification app, and large platforms would submit compliance plans to the Commission and to independent auditors. The scope covers social media, video platforms, gaming platforms and AI chatbots. On process, UPI and the IAPP report that the proposal has gone to the European Parliament and the Council; published reporting gives no date of entry into force.

US states: the definitions decide who is covered

According to NPR's investigation of September 18, similarly worded chatbot-safety bills appeared in multiple US states this year, and a Google-led industry lobbying effort supplied model language and pushed for exemptions, while lobbyists for Meta and Roblox also sought to insert their own wording. The exemption categories the report lists include products for developers or researchers, commerce and customer-service products, chatbots built into search engines, business-productivity tools, and "a feature within another software application, web interface, or computer program" — a phrase the report says appears in at least eight states' bills. Other language limits the laws to minor "account holders," which the report says could place products usable without registration outside their scope. On outcomes, the report states that Colorado's HB 26-1263 was signed into law in May and excludes 12 categories of chatbot, that Hawaii enacted its measure as Act 248 after amendments, that bills were enacted in Idaho, Georgia, Iowa, Washington, Nebraska and Oregon, and that Arizona Governor Katie Hobbs vetoed her state's bill.

A difference in verification that has to be stated

This publication's grip on the two halves above is not the same. The EU proposal is corroborated across independent reports from UPI, Euronews, Engadget, the IAPP and Dataconomy, which agree on the provisions. The US state material comes from a single outlet's original investigation: the bill texts, lobbying disclosures and communications were reviewed by NPR and have not been independently obtained by this publication, and publication by member stations is syndication of the same report, not independent verification. We therefore treat the former as cross-verified fact and, for the latter, relay only the reporting and the responses of those named — Google says it "is committed to supporting thoughtful, effective AI legislation that protects consumers while fostering innovation," Healthier Colorado says the bill "is not an industry bill in disguise," and Meta and Roblox declined to comment.

An objection already on the record

In a separate Euronews piece on September 18, the industry analyst Stanislas Marchand said that if the Kids Act requires separate European products and costly ongoing assessments, "US labs may delay launches here," adding that his feeling was that it "will further add to the transatlantic AI divide." This publication labels that for what it is: one analyst's judgment, not a research finding or an official assessment.

The limits of this account

This publication has not obtained the EU legislative text, nor the original text of any state bill or lobbying disclosure; the provisions, figures and quotations above are taken from the sources listed. We do not assess the merits of either legislative route, do not judge anyone's motives, and make no prediction about the Parliament and Council or about further legislation in the states.

「复合型危机」是怎么被拆开的：尼泊尔—西藏8月26日洪灾的归因研究说了什么、又明确没说什么

综合信源：法新社稿（经 Hong Kong Free Press 刊发） · Al Jazeera · Geographical · The Japan Times · Insurance Journal · Gulf News

先厘清事件本身

据半岛电视台与法新社稿，2026年8月26日，喜马拉雅山脉朗唐力戎峰（Langtang Lirung）峰顶附近的冰体与岩壁发生崩塌。据半岛电视台引述的研究，约1.1亿立方米的岩石与冰体落入下方谷地，引发的暴洪以每小时177公里（110英里）以上的速度推进。这不是一场典型意义上的「暴雨成灾」，而是一次地表物质的大规模位移与随之而来的洪水。

归因研究的结论：不是单一极端天气

据法新社稿与半岛电视台，研究组织「世界天气归因」（World Weather Attribution，WWA）于9月17日（周四）发布对该事件的分析，结论是这场灾难并非由单一极端天气事件造成，而是一场由长期增温、冰川退缩、永冻土消融与地质不稳定叠加而成的「复合型危机」。机制方面，据法新社稿，研究者认为持续增温抬高了喜马拉雅地区的冰点高度，使冰体与山地永冻土暴露在更高海拔、更长时段的消融条件下，从而削弱了山体坡面的稳定性。

研究给出的几组数字

据法新社稿与半岛电视台，报告称2026年7月与8月是当地有记录以来最热的月份；在洪灾发生前的数日里，气温高出30年平均值5摄氏度。另据法新社稿，2025年末的大量降雪在崩塌前增加了融水量。研究同时指出一个不确定项：2015年的7.8级地震可能进一步削弱了该处坡面，但其确切贡献仍不确定。

研究者的表述

据法新社稿，帝国理工学院气候科学教授、WWA共同创办人弗雷德里克·奥托（Friederike Otto）表示：「毫无疑问，气候变化是驱动因素之一」，并称「这些事件与人类引起的气候变化有明确关联，也明确带来了不可避免的损失与损害」。乌得勒支大学山地水文学者瓦尔特·伊默泽尔（Walter Immerzeel）表示，该地区正在经历「根本性的、不可逆的转变」，冰川退缩与永冻土退化正在削弱山体稳定性。

这类研究能做什么、不能做什么
